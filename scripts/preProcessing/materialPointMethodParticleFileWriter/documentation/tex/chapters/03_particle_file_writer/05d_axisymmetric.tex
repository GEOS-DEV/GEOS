\section{Native axisymmetric mechanics, contact and cohesive zones}
\label{sec:axisymmetric}
\index{axisymmetry}
Set \mpmcode{formulation=Axisymmetric} in PFW. The same timestep uses the
native 2D mesh with $x=r$ and $y$ axial. The geometric z coordinate and
circumferential velocity are zero. Constitutive tensors use the
$(r,y,\theta)$ basis: their zz entry is the hoop component. G and $G_0$
remain two full meridional edge generators, for a parallelogram or triangle.
No extra radius, hoop generator or vertex array is stored.

\subsection{Full-ring measures and cylindrical mapping}
PFW writes \mpmcode{spatialDimension=2} and \mpmcode{axisymmetric=1} on
\texttt{InternalMesh}. The partition retains this flag on restart; solver and
mesh must agree. The physical radial minimum is nonnegative, all particle
centers have positive radius, and radial periodicity is forbidden. Standard
exterior mesh buffer cells remain; they are not physical negative-radius
material. The solver applies radial symmetry at $r=0$.

Use full-ring physical quantities, without \texttt{planeStrainThickness}:
\[
 V_p=2\pi r_p A_p,\qquad m_p=\rho_p V_p,\qquad
 V_\Omega=\pi(r_{\max}^2-r_{\min}^2)(y_{\max}-y_{\min}).
\]
The particle center is the meridional area centroid. PFW evaluates every
emitted child's ring volume at its own radius, including cut triangles and
Cartesian-to-CPTI tessellation. Particle-sized fracture lengths use
$\sqrt{V_p/(2\pi r_p)}$; scalar user-specified lengths are unchanged.

The radius-weighted operators are
\[
 S_I=\frac{\int_A r N_I\,dA}{A r_p},\qquad
 \mathbf G_I=\frac{\int_A r\nabla N_I\,dA}{A r_p},\qquad
 T_I=\frac{\int_A N_I\,dA}{A r_p}.
\]
CPDI/CPTI integrate the material-domain interpolant of the grid basis. The
otherwise inactive third mapping-gradient slot stores $T_I$, not a z
spatial derivative. Point Q1 and cubic B-spline use $T_I=N_I/r_p$. CPDI has four corners;
CPTI has three. The cylindrical force and hoop kinematics are
\begin{align*}
 f_{Ir}&=-V_p(\sigma_{rr}G_{Ir}+\sigma_{ry}G_{Iy}+\sigma_{\theta\theta}T_I),\\
 f_{Iy}&=-V_p(\sigma_{ry}G_{Ir}+\sigma_{yy}G_{Iy}),&
 L_{\theta\theta}&=\sum_I v_{Ir}T_I.
\end{align*}
FLIP, PIC, XPIC and FMPM include the hoop term. F, physical volume and stress
power use all three tensor diagonal components; only the 2D block advances G.
Material symmetries/orientations must be compatible with no swirl.

Temporary integration geometry is uniformly shrunk about its center if a
corner would pass below $r=0$. Stored material geometry, mass and physical
volume are unchanged. Mapping, scaling diagnostics and out-of-range cleanup
use the same temporary geometry, including the axis fit; an un-fitted
stored corner below the mesh buffer is not a reason to delete a valid particle.
A center reaching $r\leq0$ is an error. Splitting uses
area fraction times $r_{\mathrm{child}}/r_{\mathrm{parent}}$ for extensive
state, with a weighted-mean correction for optional affine child velocities.
This conserves momentum, but resolving the velocity gradient can add resolved
kinetic energy, as in the existing splitter. Automatic resets preserve hoop
stretch and use $J/F_{\theta\theta}$ for planar geometry projection.

\subsection{B-spline interpolation at the axis}
Select \mpmcode{particleType=1}. On the first radial cell, write $s=r/h_r$.
The weights at $r=0,h_r,2h_r$ are
\[
 N_0=1-s+s^3/6,\qquad N_1=s-s^3/3,\qquad N_2=s^3/6.
\]
All exterior radial weights and derivatives are zero. This replaces the
exterior cardinal control value by $u_{-1}=2u_0-u_1$ in the basis itself.
The resulting weights are nonnegative, sum to one, reproduce linear fields
using physical grid coordinates, and meet the interior cubic basis with
$C^2$ continuity. Their analytic derivatives enter the same cached and
on-demand mapping routine. Small positive radii are evaluated directly from
the physical cell origin; no minimum-radius cutoff is introduced.
The hoop operator is $T_I=N_I/r_p$. With the existing radial constraint
$v_r(0)=0$, $v_r/r$ approaches the one-sided radial derivative at the axis.

This is a one-sided boundary basis, not an even/odd ghost-field extension.
It imposes zero second radial derivative of the represented field at the
boundary; it does not impose zero axial-velocity slope there. Boundary
accuracy for curved radial profiles must be checked by mesh refinement.
The change applies only at a physical revolution axis. Annular inner
boundaries, other grid faces, Cartesian F-table boundaries and internal MPI
partition faces keep their original cardinal basis and boundary handling.
No new input switch, particle geometry data or restart fields are added.

The F-table path uses the same linear continuation relation on constrained
velocity components after mapping. Substituting it into the basis changes
mass and force assembly as well, and applies a common basis to both velocity
components. Thus the two complete algorithms are not claimed equivalent.
Generalizing this basis to moving/contact/F-table boundaries is separate work.
The existing minimum-grid-size and single-partition periodic B-spline
restrictions remain. B-spline particles tagged for CZ are rejected because
the cohesive area/reference-support operator still assumes Q1 nodal hats;
ordinary contact and untagged bulk particles do not require that operator.

\subsection{Geometric surface detection}
For \mpmcode{surfaceDetection=1}, the neighbor-centroid test uses meridional
area $A_q=V_q/(2\pi r_q)$ and images at $(-r_q,y_q)$ when inside the existing
kernel support. Full-ring volume would displace the centroid outward even
in a uniformly filled section. Axis images avoid classifying the revolution
axis as a material free surface. No extra particle search or physical mass
is introduced: any contributing image has its real sample in the same
neighbor list. The existing $0.08$ times neighbor-radius threshold and
surface-flag convention are retained. Empty support does not assign a new
surface flag. Other geometric surfaces remain detectable; this change does
itself reconstruct normals or positions. The separate controls below provide
those fields and full-ring nodal-area output.
Existing flags are not automatically cleared on restart.

\subsection{Grid walls, prescribed radial stretch and normal stress control}
\label{sec:axisymmetric-grid-driving}
Axisymmetric grid faces are ordered $r-,r+,y-,y+$. Boundary types are
\mpmcode{0=Outflow}, \mpmcode{1=Symmetry}, \mpmcode{2=Moving}, and
\mpmcode{3=Contact}. A physical $r=0$ face is always Symmetry, including
BC-table transitions. A radial wall at positive radius represents a cylinder;
an axial wall represents an annular or circular platen.

Enable \mpmcode{prescribedBoundaryFTable=1} and supply
\mpmcode{fTable} rows \mpmcode{[time,Frr,Fyy,1]} to prescribe the two grid
stretches. The time interpolation choices remain Linear, Cosine and Smoothstep.
The first stretches are one; subsequent prescribed stretches must be positive.
Grid coordinates use the finite ratio $F_{n+1}/F_n$ while wall velocity uses
the existing endpoint rate $L=\dot F/F$ at the endpoint wall position.
The radial map is about $r=0$; both faces of an annulus share the same radial
scale. Axial scaling is about $y=0$. Independent wall translations, shear of
the grid and swirl are not introduced.

The third entry of \mpmcode{domainF}, \mpmcode{fTable}, and the history column
\mpmcode{F22} describes an inactive grid coordinate and remains one. It is
not particle $F_{\theta\theta}$. Particle hoop rate remains $v_r/r$, and
hoop stretch is advanced by the existing particle constitutive kinematics.
The grid volume scales as $F_{rr}^{2}F_{yy}$. A fixed Symmetry face away from
the origin is incompatible with driving that direction; use Moving or Contact.
Moving walls impose their normal velocity bilaterally. Contact walls apply a
compressive normal impulse only when the mapped surface intersects the wall
and approaches it relative to its prescribed velocity. With no explicit
surface position, the existing mapped center-of-volume gap estimate is used.
Axisymmetric wall friction opposes the single meridional slip component and
is capped by $\mu$ times the actual normal impulse; it cannot reverse slip or
act on a separated wall. This is an inelastic normal wall law; no restitution
extension is added. Full-ring mass already supplies the circumference factor.

Set \mpmcode{stressControl=[1,0,0]} for radial control, or
\mpmcode{[0,1,0]} for axial control; both components may be controlled.
\mpmcode{stressTable} rows are \mpmcode{[time,sigma\_rr,sigma\_yy,unused]}.
Compression is negative. Each controlled component measures
\[
 \overline{\sigma}_{ii}^{\rm domain}
  =\frac{\sum_p V_p\sigma_{ii,p}}
  {\pi(R_{\rm out}^2-R_{\rm in}^2)H},\qquad i=r,y.
\]
The denominator includes void. Radial feedback is $\sigma_{rr}$ alone,
not outer-wall pressure and not $\sigma_{rr}+\sigma_{\theta\theta}$, which
would be conjugate to uniform radial scaling in a work calculation.
A control entry of one overrides that direction's F-table column. Entry two
retains the existing one-sided limit on prescribed loading. The legacy PID
controller and \mpmcode{stressControlAdaptiveSeek=1} are supported; adaptive
seek still requires control entries zero or one. Controller rate caps use
absolute wall distance from the scaling origin, including annular grids.
At least one Moving or Contact face is required in each driven direction.
Axially periodic domains may remain periodic when axial wall driving is off.

\mpmcode{prescribedBcTable=1} supports rows
\mpmcode{[time,r-,r+,y-,y+]}; seven-column padded rows are also accepted.
Tables start at zero, increase in time and retain type one at a physical
axis. Their first row supplies the initial types. A wall may be released to
Outflow after its prescribed F ramp ends, provided stress control in that
direction is off. Constant transverse velocities remain available on Moving faces when
compatible with no swirl and axis regularity. Existing cyclic transverse slots
are unchanged: the axial component on a radial face is slot zero, and the
radial component on an axial face is slot one. The latter must be zero when
the cap includes $r=0$.

Moving walls support FLIP/PIC/XPIC/FMPM; Contact walls support FLIP/PIC/XPIC.
FMPM contact-wall increments remain unsupported. The separate
\mpmcode{prescribedFTable} periodic affine-bulk path and the
\mpmcode{DeformationUpdate} event remain blocked. No new B-spline boundary
basis is introduced here: the physical axis keeps its one-sided basis and
other walls retain their existing constrained-velocity continuation.

Face reactions are full-ring scalar component sums. Cylindrical wall area is
$2\pi R H$, and platen area is $\pi(R_{\rm out}^2-R_{\rm in}^2)$; neither
requires a fictitious thickness. \mpmcode{boxAverageHistory} reports material
volume-averaged stress, so multiply it by selected material volume divided by
the full domain volume before comparing it with the controller target.
\mpmcode{examples/axisymmetricGridDriving} provides prescribed-stretch,
radial-stress and mixed-control modes, a runner and a history post-processor.
Tests compile production table/grid/wall/controller code through CPU adapters
and execute full PFW generation with emulated collectives. A complete GEOS
build, real MPI/restart execution and dynamics convergence remain run-time
validation tasks.

\subsection{Supported scope and deferred integrations}
The implementation supports ordinary contact between separate body groups,
Q1 point, cubic B-spline, CPDI and CPTI particles, meridional grid walls, and
compatible body-force, initial-velocity and material initialization.
It supports DFG contact-surface insertion. It explicitly rejects finite-thickness
polymer CZ, SPH/overlap corrections, crack-tip detection, profileHistory slice normalization,
CZ-tagged B-spline particles and periodic affine-bulk driving.
BC tables, boundary F tables and radial/axial normal stress control are supported
as described below. PFW rejects surface traction generation;
direct GEOS particle tractions are interpreted as already integrated full-ring
forces. \mpmcode{ConfiningPressure} supports uniform, box and sphere modes;
\mpmcode{BoreholePressure} selects $r<r_0$ independently of axial position.
Their background stresses include the hoop component; pressure is retained
after the event and across restart. Most geometry-changing/healing events are rejected. The prescribed-interface
\mpmcode{ReferenceCohesiveZones} event is supported with explicit or reconstructed surface data.
\mpmcode{FrictionCoefficientSwap} is also supported: scalar/table coefficients
are updated by its existing one-shot handler, with no geometry-dependent change.

Built-in area-only Weibull objects are rejected: a statistical cell needs
$2\pi\int_A r\,dA$, before minimum-volume clamping. A custom wrapper can
implement \texttt{setAxisymmetric()}; explicit StrengthScale values are not
rescaled. Plane-strain thickness binding remains unchanged. Weibull and
localized hoop-driven cracking are deferred because circumferential
localization requires a treatment beyond the current symmetry assumption.

Silo/VTK output continues to show meridional quad/triangle glyphs at z=0.
Stress zz is hoop stress; volume, mass and reactions are full-ring values.
Reaction-history length\_z is zero; do not use Cartesian thickness-based
force-to-pressure conversions.
No automatic revolved output mesh is generated. ParticleColor is retained.

\subsection{Pressure regions and traction interpretation}
Pressure events select grid nodes in current physical coordinates. Cutoffs
are fixed in space and do not track material boundaries. With $x=r$, $y$
axial and positive compressive pressure $p$, the selections are:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
Event/mode & Selected nodes \\
\hline
\texttt{BoreholePressure} & $r^2<r_0^2$, independent of $y$ \\
Confinement, sphere & $r^2+y^2>R^2$ \\
Confinement, box & outside $[r_{\min},r_{\max}]\times[y_{\min},y_{\max}]$ \\
Confinement, uniform & every node
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The sphere is centered at the origin and revolves about $y$. Box XML retains
three entries per corner, but z is ignored. Boundary equalities are inside
the pressure-free region. Confinement overwrites borehole pressure if both
select a node. Each kind has one stored region; successive events of the same
kind replace that shared region rather than adding another load.

For a hollow cylinder with inner/outer radii $a,b$, choose
$a<r_0<r_1<b$ with margins appropriate to boundary support and deformation.
Use \mpmcode{boreholeRadius}=$r_0$ and a box whose maximum radial entry is
$r_1$; its minimum radial and both axial bounds must extend beyond the entire
grid, including buffers, to select only $r>r_1$. For example, a 10--20 mm
annulus in a millimetre-scale grid can use:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=XML]
<BoreholePressure name="innerPressure" startTime="0" endTime="0.005"
  boreholeRadius="0.0105" startPressure="0" endPressure="100000" interpType="2"/>
<ConfiningPressure name="outerPressure" startTime="0" endTime="0.005"
  confiningPressureBoxMin="{-1,-1,0}" confiningPressureBoxMax="{0.0195,1,0}"
  startPressure="0" endPressure="20000" interpType="2"/>
\end{lstlisting}
Put these in PFW's \mpmcode{mpmEventsString} with \mpmcode{useEvents=1}.
For spherical exclusion, replace the box corners with
\mpmcode{confiningPressureSphereRadius}. Uniform mode instead sets
\mpmcode{uniformPressure=1} and omits all exclusion geometry.

The cylindrical weak form uses $\boldsymbol\sigma^{\rm bg}=-p\mathbf I$,
including hoop pressure. Volumes already contain $2\pi r$; do not multiply
the prescribed pressure by circumference. No surface flags, reconstructed
normals or particle traction records are needed. The spatial mask can load
any free/interface boundary represented by its selected support, including
end-face bands and cracks. It is not a named external-surface selector.

Axisymmetric ramps assign the exact \mpmcode{endPressure} on the step reaching
their end, then hold it. Zero-duration events assign their end pressure.
The general event scheduler and Cartesian sampling convention are unchanged.
Both pressure states are saved for restart; completed events remain completed.
Direct GEOS particle tractions are already integrated full-ring forces;
generic PFW per-area traction integration remains unsupported.

\path{examples/axisymmetricPressureRegions} supplies the annular example.

Set \mpmcode{AXIS\_PRESSURE\_CASE=sphere} for the spherical variant.
Both have a runner and history comparison against small-strain static
references. Production-excerpt tests check region
selection, overlap precedence, inner/outer full-ring resultants, pressure work,
hoop balance, input validation and ramp holds. Full writer runs compare output
at 1/3 emulated ranks. Full GEOS dynamics and restart remain target checks.

\subsection{Ordinary contact between bodies}
For ordinary contact without DFG, assign separate nonnegative
\mpmcode{ContactGroup} values and set
\mpmcode{enableContact=1}, with \mpmcode{damageFieldPartitioning=0} and
\mpmcode{overlapCorrection=Off}. Existing normal-selection, gap-gating and
Coulomb-friction options are retained. Nodal mass and momentum already use
full-ring measures, so the contact force/impulse law needs no extra $2\pi r$.
The shared boundary-normal reconstruction is
\[
 \boldsymbol b_I=\sum_p V_p\bigl(G_{Ir}+T_I,\ G_{Iy},\ 0\bigr).
\]
The added radial term follows from
$\nabla(rN_I)=r\nabla N_I+N_I\boldsymbol e_r$ and removes the radial bias
that would otherwise remain inside a fully occupied nodal support. It does
not prescribe extra circumference weighting for supplied unit normals.
The same assembled normals serve ordinary and FMPM contact.

Explicit PFW surface positions can be used with
\mpmcode{useSurfacePositionForContact=1}; otherwise the existing approximate
centre-of-mass gap criterion applies. Reconstructed surfaces and nodal areas
are supported as described in Section~\ref{sec:axisymmetric-surfaces}; moving
walls use Section~\ref{sec:axisymmetric-grid-driving}. Overlap correction remains
unsupported. Contact and slip have only radial and axial components.

\path{examples/axisymmetricContact} supplies two elastic cylinders with separate
contact groups and mixed CPDI/CPTI particles. Each has radius 10 mm and length
8 mm; they approach at $\pm1$ m/s across a 2-mm gap. The example includes input
checks, a runner and history plots. \mpmcode{AXIS\_CONTACT\_UPDATE=FMPM} selects
FMPM(2); the default is FLIP. Standalone tests check normal cancellation and
orientation on axial, cylindrical and inclined interfaces, axis behavior,
friction limits, separation, momentum exchange and contact dissipation. Writer
checks use emulated collectives; full GEOS contact dynamics and MPI/restart
validation remain target-machine tasks.

The uniform pressure bath used in the bonded jacketed example can also load
newly exposed internal surfaces if the materials are separated into contact
groups. A spatial exclusion can protect internal gaps only while their nodal
support remains outside the selected pressure region. General exterior-only
loading for a deforming sealed jacket remains a separate integration task.


\subsection{Axisymmetric DFG contact-surface insertion}
Set \mpmcode{damageFieldPartitioning=1} and \mpmcode{enableContact=1}.
The existing DFG field assignment, separability law and defaults remain in
use. For $n_g$ contact groups, DFG supplies two slots per group with field
index $\mathrm{side}\,n_g+\mathrm{group}$. Ordinary contact evaluates the
occupied field pairs in both the standard and FMPM paths. This does not
create cohesive bonds or generalize the CZ field-pair representation.

Damage is a scalar on the meridional section. Its reconstruction uses
\[
 A_q=\frac{V_q}{2\pi r_q},\qquad
 \bar d(\boldsymbol x)=\frac{\sum_q A_q d_q K(\boldsymbol x-\boldsymbol x_q)}
                              {\sum_q A_q K(\boldsymbol x-\boldsymbol x_q)},\qquad
 \nabla\bar d=\frac{\sum_q A_q(d_q-\bar d)\nabla K_q}{\sum_q A_q K_q}.
\]
Use the existing compact cubic kernel and neighbor set. Each sample also
contributes its image at $(-r_q,y_q)$ when within support. This even radial
extension makes the radial derivative zero at the axis; it creates no
particles or additional physical mass. The existing surface-as-damage
convention is retained. Particle positions and volumes are current values.
Only this reconstruction changes measure: mapped damage, quality tensors,
volume fractions, nodal mass, momentum and contact forces retain full-ring
weights. No additional circumference factor enters the contact law.

The existing separability criterion decides whether same-group fields remain
tied or can open and recontact. Its development is separate from this
axisymmetric adaptation. Surface reconstruction can be requested separately;
a newly inserted DFG surface without reconstructed data uses the approximate
centre-of-mass gap criterion with \mpmcode{useSurfacePositionForContact=0}.
The current partition uses one reference direction per grid node and two
slots per group; this patch does not add unresolved crack-junction topology.
Only cracks whose revolution is axisymmetric are representable. Localized
longitudinal hoop fractures and swirl remain outside the formulation.

\path{examples/axisymmetricDFG} contains four isolation cases: an intact
cylinder, the same cylinder with a prescribed damaged central band, three
approaching contact groups, and the three groups with damage in the central
body. The first pair initially moves away from the midplane. There is no
applied tensile end traction, new damage-evolution law, explicit internal
normal, or CZ activation. Select with \mpmcode{AXIS\_DFG\_CASE}; use
\mpmcode{AXIS\_DFG\_UPDATE=FMPM} for FMPM(2), otherwise FLIP. Particle checks,
runner and tracer postprocessing are included. The prescribed damage band exercises the existing separability criterion; it does not introduce a new fracture law. Standalone kernel and writer checks do not replace target GEOS dynamics, real MPI/restart or GPU tests.

\subsection{Prescribed axisymmetric cohesive interfaces}
Use exactly two contact groups, 0 and 1, and disable DFG. A
\mpmcode{CohesiveZoneRegion} selects particles through its tag; surface
particles use \mpmcode{SurfaceFlag=3}. PFW must include ContactGroup, CZTag,
SurfaceFlag, SurfaceNormal and SurfacePosition columns. The surface vectors
are meridional. Supply a nonzero normal or request reconstruction before
activation (Section~\ref{sec:axisymmetric-surfaces}). Set \mpmcode{useEvents=1} on the
solver and activate the region with
\mpmcode{ReferenceCohesiveZones}, using \mpmcode{computeNormalsAndPositions=0} for explicit geometry or one
for solver reconstruction.
The event dispatcher defaults to disabled; declaring the event and marking
surface candidates alone does not set active particle cohesive flags.
Each reference event requests initialization once, using an internal
\mpmcode{referenceApplied} flag saved across restart. A time window alone is
insufficient: timestep growth can admit repeated activation and overwrite the
bonded reference after separation begins. Dependency scheduling is distinct
from activation, so its \mpmcode{hasStarted} flag is not used for this purpose.
Finite event end times still disable the region; a separate reference event
can intentionally initialize it again. This fix leaves general event
scheduling unchanged and applies to all formulations. Rebuild and restart a
case affected by repeated initialization from time zero.
Uncoupled, Coupled and Bicrystal traction--separation laws are supported.
Finite-thickness Polymer and SurfaceInformedPolymer CZ models remain rejected:
they need hoop stretch in their constitutive deformation reconstruction.

At activation, the reference nodal area is
\[
 A_{I,\mathrm{CZ}}=\int_{\Gamma}2\pi r N_I\,ds.
\]
The reconstructed local line is clipped to nodal support and $r\geq0$, split
at Q1 hat-function kinks, and integrated exactly with two-point Gauss rules.
The optional region \mpmcode{czVolumeNormalization=1} uses the matching support
volume $\int 2\pi r N_I\,dA$; at an axis node it is
$2\pi h_r^2h_y/6$. Multiplication by the node radius alone is incorrect.
Finite interface ends retain the existing local-line occupancy approximation;
verify area and reaction convergence with refinement.

Current area uses the full three-dimensional relative cofactor,
\[
 H=F F_{\mathrm{CZ}}^{-1},\qquad
 \mathbf a_I=\langle\operatorname{cof}H\rangle_I
              A_{I,\mathrm{CZ}}\mathbf n_{I,\mathrm{CZ}}.
\]
Thus axial and cylindrical areas include hoop stretch. Reference normals are
normalized. Re-basing $F_{\mathrm{CZ}}$ during an F reset preserves H and area.
Both FOnly and FAndGeometry retain the cached cohesive anchor. TypeB selects
that anchor by intersecting the normal ray with the unscaled quad/triangle
material domain; TypeA uses the supplied surface offset and Nodal retains the
reference-grid affine reconstruction. These choices change the displacement
reconstruction, not the reference-area integration.

The law uses opening and meridional slip; force is traction times the current
full-ring area once. With \mpmcode{preventCZInterpenetration=1}, ordinary contact
handles compression. A particle retains cohesive forces while any cached CZ
node remains intact; complete failure releases it to ordinary contact.
Contact handoff remains particle-based, not an independently tracked per-node
crack front. Active/damaged cohesive surface particles are not subdivided.
Scaling/reset options preserve their surface normals and positions.

\mpmcode{gridCohesiveArea} now contains current area vectors, and
\mpmcode{gridCohesiveForce} contains forces, at cached reference-grid locations.
Meridional Silo/VTK output needs no extra thickness conversion.
\path{examples/axisymmetricCZ} provides mixed CPDI/CPTI elastic and debond
variants, a runner, input checks and tracer plots. Standalone tests compile
production area, CZ-law, relative-F, reset and gather excerpts. An event test
also compiles the reference event and scheduler/handler excerpts to check
adaptive timesteps, dependency delays and restart-wrapper state. Writer tests
exercise FLIP/FMPM with one and three emulated ranks. Full GEOS dynamics,
actual MPI/restart and GPU execution remain target tests. See
The standalone adapter checks described above are not a substitute for full GEOS dynamics, real MPI/restart or GPU validation.


\subsection{Reconstructed surfaces, nodal area and surface tension}
\label{sec:axisymmetric-surfaces}
\mpmcode{computeParticleSurfaceNormalsAndPositions=1} requests a one-time
pass before mechanical P2G. It updates Surface/Cohesive/DamagedCohesive particles;
use PFW flags or \mpmcode{surfaceDetection=1}. Existing nonzero normals are
preserved unless \mpmcode{overwriteExistingNormalsAndPositions=1}; active
CZ reference geometry is always retained. The existing inverse-F/cofactor
transforms update reference offsets and normals.

All mechanical interpolation types share a transient geometric Q1 projection
from current unified quad/triangle geometry. It splits polygons at grid cells
and integrates $2\pi rN_I$ exactly on each piece, using existing temporary
integration-domain scaling/axis fitting. Mechanical CPTI interpolation is not
used for this auxiliary volume: it would give a different capacity from Q1
near boundaries. Out-of-local-grid glyphs use a Q1 point fallback.
The new \mpmcode{gridSurfaceMaterialVolume} is transient nodal state, not a
new particle geometry representation. Owned particles scatter once; shared
sums and owner-to-ghost reconstructed planes are synchronized.

The normal integral is $\sum_p V_p(G_{Ir}+T_I,G_{Iy},0)$.
\mpmcode{DFGAndVolumeIntegration} uses this direction.
\mpmcode{LogisticRegression} refines it using the dominant neighboring field,
meridional area weights $V_p/(2\pi r_p)$, dimensionless coordinates and axis
images. Free surfaces and failed fits use the volume-gradient normal. Each
field's own volume determines its offset, so a gap is retained. Neither field
partitioning nor separability criteria change.

For a unit normal $\boldsymbol n$ and node-relative offset $d$, solve
\[
 V_I=\int_{\boldsymbol n\cdot(\boldsymbol x-\boldsymbol x_I)\le d}
       2\pi rN_I\,dA,\qquad
 A_I=\int_{\boldsymbol n\cdot(\boldsymbol x-\boldsymbol x_I)=d}
       2\pi rN_I\,ds.
\]
Both use Q1 support clipped at $r=0$ and physical Symmetry faces. A through-node
plane is not generally half-full near the axis. The line integral is exact
for the reconstructed local plane; curvature/corners remain approximation
errors. The axis has zero physical area. Convert each nodal plane to a
particle-relative normal distance before averaging, to avoid the
radius-weighted interpolation-centroid bias.

\mpmcode{computeNodalArea=1} repeats grid reconstruction each step without
repeatedly replacing particle reference surfaces. \mpmcode{gridSurfaceArea}
contains full-ring area per field; the field-0 log is globally summed over
owned nodes. A shared interface occurs separately in both fields.
The \mpmcode{ReferenceCohesiveZones} event accepts
\mpmcode{computeNormalsAndPositions=1}. A region reconstruction request is
scheduled before that enabled region initializes, using the solver method.
PFW permits zero initial CZ normals with a reconstruction request; retain the
normal/position columns and arrange reconstruction by interface activation.
Existing two-group CZ and CZ-tagged B-spline restrictions remain.

Enable \mpmcode{enableSurfaceTension=1} and provide
\mpmcode{surfaceTensionPairs=[[A,B,gamma],...]} in PFW; $B=-1$ means void.
Coefficients are constant force/length, with ContactGroup indices. Void color
is geometric field volume divided by Q1 ring support volume; pair color is
$V_A/(V_A+V_B)$ on each existing DFG side. B-spline color gathers use even
reflection at symmetry faces. The mechanical gradients retain the particle's
actual interpolation. With $\boldsymbol n=\nabla c/|\nabla c|$,
\[
 \boldsymbol\tau=\gamma|\nabla c|(\boldsymbol I-\boldsymbol n\otimes\boldsymbol n),
 \quad f_{Ir}=-V_p(\tau_{rr}G_{Ir}+\tau_{ry}G_{Iy}+\tau_{\theta\theta}T_I),
 \quad f_{Iy}=-V_p(\tau_{ry}G_{Ir}+\tau_{yy}G_{Iy}).
\]
The hoop stress supplies circumferential curvature; the third mapping slot
is never a color-gradient component. Axisymmetric material/void stress is
multiplied by two because only the material half of the diffuse layer is
sampled. Material/material layers sample both sides without this multiplier.
Full-ring volume already contains the circumference factor. The existing
capillary timestep limit applies; no surface flags or reconstruction switch
are required for capillary force alone. Cartesian force formulas are retained.

This diffuse formulation is not exactly balanced against initialized Laplace
pressure. Check residual velocities and pressure/area under refinement; no
prescribed contact-angle or Marangoni model is introduced. Severe domain
scaling, corners and under-resolved multiple surfaces reduce accuracy.

$A=4\pi$, $p=0.02$, with $R=H=1$ and $\gamma=0.01$.
