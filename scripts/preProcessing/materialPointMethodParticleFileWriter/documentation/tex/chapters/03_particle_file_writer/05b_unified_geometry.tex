\section{Unified material geometry and schema 2}
\label{sec:unified-particle-geometry}
\index{geometryReset}
\index{particleGeometry}

Every particle stores a shape tag and two minimal full-edge matrices:
\mpmcode{particleGeometryReference} ($G_0$) and
\mpmcode{particleGeometryCurrent} ($G$). Generators are rows in memory and files;
the corresponding column-vector equation $G=F G_0$ is stored as $G=G_0F^T$.
That equality need not hold after an F-only reset. The current geometry is
advanced by the physical increment, $G^{n+1}=G^n(\Delta F)^T$, independently of
constitutive reference changes. Vertices and integration geometry are temporary.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.18\linewidth}p{0.49\linewidth}p{0.20\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule Shape & Generator and vertex convention & Initial measure\\\midrule
Box (0) & Three full edges; $c\pm g_1/2\pm g_2/2\pm g_3/2$ & $\det G$\\
Wedge (1) & Two triangle edges plus thickness; triangle offsets $\pm g_3/2$ & $\det G/2$\\
Tet (2) & Three edges from $v_0$; $v_0=c-(g_1+g_2+g_3)/4$ & $\det G/6$\\
2D quad (3) & Two 2-component edges; centered parallelogram & $\det G$\\
2D tri (4) & Two 2-component edges; $v_0=c-(g_1+g_2)/3$ & $\det G/2$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}

The matrices occupy 144 bytes per 3D particle, plus the shape tag. This retains
the previous two-matrix box cost and removes the extra 72-byte glyph-reference
matrix for native simplices. Native 2D storage uses 64 bytes for two
$2\times2$ matrices. Its physical volume is the listed geometric area times
the domain thickness $t$; see Section~\ref{sec:native-true-2d}.

\subsection{Reset policy and integration geometry}
\mpmcode{geometryReset=FOnly} (default) changes F and preserves G and $G_0$.
\mpmcode{geometryReset=FAndGeometry} also sets $G=G_0F_{\rm new}^T$, preserving
$G_0$. The policy applies to automatic and healing resets and is independent of
\mpmcode{domainResetType}, which chooses the new F. Startup thermal initialization
may establish $G_0$ from the initial F; subdivision assigns smaller child
references. Neither numerical reset policy changes particle mass or physical
volume. Material geometry measure can therefore differ from physical volume
after a deliberate reset, especially after healing changes reference volume.

CPDI mapping copies G into a temporary integration matrix $\widehat G$, applies
Homel or VP-Hencky scaling, and uses local half edges $r=\widehat G/2$ in the
four/eight-corner basis/gradient formula. No integration matrix is stored per
particle. Cached and on-demand mapping, neighbor support, and CPDI boundary
checks use the same projection. The projections are generally anisotropic;
one scalar cannot encode them. Full-edge VP-Hencky feasibility is
$M\leq(D/\sqrt d)^d$. Physical G, $G_0$, F, mass and volume are unchanged by
integration scaling. SinglePoint and SinglePointBSpline sample at the center
and retain the same material geometry for output and splitting.

Splitting uses unscaled G and applies identical child maps to G and $G_0$.
Boxes and parallelograms bisect generators; triangles and tets bisect their
longest current edge; 3D wedges split a triangle edge or their thickness. A bounded pass has at most four
children in plane strain or eight in 3D. Mass and physical volumes use child
fractions. Applied surface forces are partitioned, and kinetic energy is
recomputed after the optional velocity correction. Surface-reference offsets
are translated in the constitutive F frame, which can differ from $G_0$ after
FOnly. The generic splitter continues to exclude cohesive particles. CZ TypeB selects its cached
anchor by intersecting the current surface-normal ray with the actual material
shape at CZ initialization; TypeA uses the supplied surface offset. Subsequent
relative-F transport and Nodal behavior retain their existing roles. The
TypeB anchor is a boundary estimate, not an explicit interface-facet record.

\subsection{PFW inputs and the one file contract}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
pfw['particleGeometry'] = 'fromRefinement'  # default: box
pfw['surfaceRefinementMethod'] = 'simplex'
pfw['surfaceRefinementLevel'] = 2
pfw['geometryReset'] = 'FOnly'              # or FAndGeometry
\end{lstlisting}
Old Python input names are normalized in PFW: \texttt{particleReferenceGeometry}
maps \texttt{equivalentBox} to \texttt{box}; \texttt{fromRefinement} retains cut
shapes. Both old \texttt{particleGeometryFormat} values emit schema 2.
\texttt{RVector} field requests are consumed. \texttt{particleDomainOutput} and
\texttt{plotUnscaledParticles} are accepted by PFW but no longer emitted to XML;
Silo and VTK always display current material geometry.

Every particle row requires \texttt{GeometryVersion=2}, \texttt{GeometryShape},
\texttt{ParticleVolume}, and dimension-dependent full-edge components: four XX/XY/YX/YY in 2D,
nine XX through ZZ in 3D. ID and active position components are required;
PositionZ is zero or may be omitted in native 2D. The reader
rejects partial/old schemas, nonfinite or inverted geometry, and initial measure
disagreement above relative tolerance $10^{-9}$. Initial wedges are xy triangles
extruded in z. There are no R-vector, optional glyph, or integration-width
columns. GEOS XML and restart backward compatibility are intentionally removed:
regenerate particles/XML and start a new run. New restarts carry both geometry
matrices and require restart geometry version 3. The particle file version
remains 2; shape tags and dimension select the component layout.

\subsection{Scope and verification}
CPTI is supported for native triangles/tetrahedra, with corner weights $1/3$ or $1/4$ and
barycentric gradients computed from current integration geometry. CPTI uses
uniform diameter clipping rather than the box feasibility cap; see
Section~\ref{subsec:cpti-cpdi2-placeholders}. CPDI requires parallelograms/boxes, while PFW cuts
support point types 0/1 or CPTI type 3. A simplex requesting VP-Hencky F selection
uses the isotropic-polar reset algorithm. Warped CPDI2, plane stress and axisymmetry remain future work.

Standalone tests exercise production geometry, subdivision, and CPDI kernel
bodies with CPU adapters. Splitting tests cover unequal child fractions,
surface-force transfer, constitutive history, melt state, and both reset policies. Full-writer checks include independent processes with explicitly
emulated collectives. A full GEOS build, real MPI migration/restart, GPU execution,
and coupled CZ regression remain integration checks. See
\path{UNIFIED_PARTICLE_GEOMETRY.md} and the complete
\path{examples/unifiedGeometry/pfw_input_unified_geometry.py} input.
