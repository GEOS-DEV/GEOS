# AS screenout study: status and next tests

Handoff for running the next set of tests on HPC. It covers what the model is, what the runs so far
showed, and the test plan. Date: 2026-09-27. Branch: `feat/frankfei/ammoniumSulfateHydrofrac`.

## Goal

Reproduce, in a 2D near-wellbore phase-field model, the screenout seen in lab tests with ammonium
sulfate (AS) fracking fluid. Warm, saturated AS fluid cools in the crack, AS precipitates, the
precipitate clogs the crack, and the pressure drop along the crack grows, more so as the crack advances
into colder rock. The lab signature to reproduce is a higher injection pressure with AS than with water.

**Every result is judged as AS minus a matched water control.** The water control is the same deck with
zero reactive surface area. Absolute curves mostly reflect mesh, boundary and permeability choices; only
the difference is the screenout.

## Model: eigenstrain hybrid

The sequential Biot fixed-stress model (`AS_wellbore_base.xml`, `PorousReactive…` solids) fails within a
few steps of breakdown no matter the settings. That path is parked as a solver problem; don't spend
runs on it. All screenout work uses the eigenstrain hybrid (`EigenStrainReactive…` solids):

- Pore pressure loads the skeleton with b = 1 − g(d)(1 − b₀). With `intactPressureCoefficient` b₀ = 0,
  intact rock is not unloaded by pressure seeping in; a cracked cell carries the full pressure.
- Porosity changes only through reaction and cracking, never through strain:
  φ = φ₀ − Σ(v_r − v_r,0) + d̄·max(φ_c − φ₀, 0), with `crackPorosity` φ_c and d̄ the element-averaged damage.
  Flow therefore does not depend on mechanics, and the sequential split converges in one outer iteration
  per step.
- Precipitate enters the mechanics as an eigenstrain on the solid (see open question 2).
- Reactive surface area is gated by damage (`surfaceAreaDamageExponent` = 1), so precipitation
  happens only in cracked rock.
- Permeability: `DamageCloggingPermeability`, k = k₀·exp(c·d)·(1 − θ)³, with θ the clogged pore fraction.
- Precipitation is driven by a prescribed cooling field: the fluid is saturated at 60 °C and injected into
  25 °C rock. Temperature is a 4D table, not a solved field.

- **Geometry:** a 150 mm square, bore radius 6.35 mm, no notch. The crack nucleates at the wall at ±90°.
- **Rock:** E = 40 GPa, ν = 0.23, φ₀ = 0.05, k₀ = 5e-18 m².
- **Phase field:** l = 0.5 mm, Gc = 12.5 J/m² (test 1b, now the deck default), AT1, `hybridSplit` = 1,
  η (`viscousRegularizationCoeff`) = 1 Pa·s. All the results below used l = 0.1 mm, Gc = 2.5 J/m².
- **Crack:** `damageDependenceConstant` = 8 (crack k ≈ 1.5e-14 m², the cubic-law value for a ~5 µm opening), `crackPorosity` = 0.1.
- **Fluid:** compressibility 1e-8 /Pa, about 20 times that of real water.
- **Injection:** 1.67e-6 kg/s into the 1 mm slice.
- **Outer boundary:** faces held at p = 0 (`outerPressure`), i.e. drained.
- **Time steps:** dt 0.1 s until 17 s, then 0.01 s; maxTime 40 s.

## Results so far (un-notched, all AS/water pairs)

| pair | settings | breakdown AS / water | what happened |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | c = 5, area 100 | 18.07 MPa, 19.19 s / 18.05 MPa, 19.17 s | Slow crack (~4.5 mm/s) held back by strong tip suction (−10 MPa, see below). AS − water borehole pressure grows from +12 kPa (21 s) to +99 kPa (26.8 s) with θ ≤ 0.015: the right shape but negligible size. Crack reach identical. |
| 2 (= baseline) | c = 8, area 2000 | **18.88 MPa, 20.33 s** / 18.05 MPa, 19.17 s | AS delays breakdown by 1.2 s and 0.83 MPa, probably through the reaction eigenstrain (θ ≈ 2e-4 at breakdown, so not clogging). No suction. θ reaches 0.085 near the bore by 26 s and keeps rising. But the crack reaches about 50 mm within 1 s of breakdown, branches at ~60 mm and connects to the drained boundary. Borehole pressure then drops to ~1 MPa for both fluids, and the screenout signal is lost. |
| 3 | pair 2 + η = 1000, maxTime 40 | 19.13 MPa, 20.70 s / pending | The crack is only slightly slower (47 mm at 22 s, 63 mm at 24.6 s) and still reaches the boundary. θ grows at the same rate. The water run was being redone on the laptop when this was written. |

What the runs show:

1. **The crack outruns the clog by 10–20×.** θ near the bore grows ~0.015 per second, so reaching θ ≈ 0.3
   (a 3× permeability drop) needs ~20 s. The crack crosses the domain in 1–2 s. Precipitation is limited by
   kinetics, not by mass: the fluid still carries about half of its excess AS when it leaves the cold zone.
   So more reactive area or a longer residence time both raise θ.
2. **The run-away is probably driven by energy stored in the fluid.** With compressibility 1e-8, the fluid in
   the bore holds about 14 s of injection at 18 MPa (real water: about 0.6 s), and releases it into the crack
   at breakdown. On top of that, nucleating at a smooth wall needs a high breakdown pressure, which leaves
   excess energy once the crack starts.
3. **The drained edge short-circuits the crack.** Once the conductive crack reaches p = 0 at 75 mm, borehole
   pressure is set only by how well the crack conducts to the edge, and clogging barely changes it.
4. **Tip suction comes from a poorly conducting crack combined with crack porosity.** With c = 5 the crack
   can't deliver the fluid that fills new crack pore space (Δφ · band width · crack speed), so pressure falls
   to −10 MPa (there is no cavitation floor). With c = 8 it is gone.
5. **Branching** appears when the crack has excess driving force (fast growth, or near the boundary). The
   slow crack in pair 1 never branched. It is not caused by the chemistry.
6. **The mesh under-resolves the crack band.** h/l is 1.6 at the wall and about 5 at 60 mm. Band width
   therefore depends on the mesh, and with it the crack's conductivity, storage and reactive surface.

## How to judge a pair: the screenout panel

Only the time before the crack reaches the outer boundary counts. For each AS/water pair, produce:

1. **Borehole pressure vs time**, plus AS − water. Also plot log(net pressure) against log(time since
   breakdown), a Nolte–Smith plot: a positive, rising slope is the screenout signature.
2. **Crack length vs time**, AS vs water. With screenout the AS crack slows or arrests, or widens or branches.
3. **Pressure drop across the clogged zone vs time**: p at 8 mm minus p at 30 mm along the crack, divided by
   the total drop along the crack. The AS fraction should rise while the water fraction stays flat.
4. **Space–time map of θ(r, t)** with the crack tip overlaid. A clog far behind the tip is a near-wellbore
   choke; a clog that follows the tip is a tip screenout.

VTK fields used: `rockSolid_damage` (average over quadrature points), `rockPerm_cloggedPoreFraction`,
`pressure`, `temperature`, `rockPerm_permeability`, `rockPorosity_porosity`, `logPrimarySpeciesConcentration`,
`elementCenter`. Borehole pressure: `sourcePressureHistory.hdf5`, mean of the non-zero entries.
Crack cells: d > 0.5 inside a narrow cone about +y (|x| < 0.4·y + 1 mm). Saturation index:
Ω = exp(2·ln m_NH4 + ln m_SO4 − ln K(T)), with ln K linear in 1/T between 6.6495 (25 °C) and 7.0575 (60 °C).

## Next tests

Run each AS deck with its water control, one pair at a time. Start from the baseline decks above.

### Stage 1: slow the crack for physical reasons

| test | change (rock / fluid in `AS_wellbore_eigenstrain.xml`) | question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1a | fluid `compressibility` 1e-8 → **4.5e-10** | Is the run-away driven by energy stored in the fluid? |
| 1b | `rockSolid` `lengthScale` 1e-4 → **5e-4** and `defaultCriticalFractureEnergy` 2.5 → **12.5** (same ψ_c ∝ Gc/l). **Already applied to the repo deck**, and running as a pair on the laptop (`E_hybrid_nonotch_c8A2000_l05` / `…_water_c8_l05`). | Does higher toughness slow growth? This also brings h/l ≤ 1 out to ~50 mm without remeshing. |
| 1c | 1a + 1b together, if both help | The best physical slowdown. |

- **For 1a, the time stepping must change.** Storage drops ~20×, so breakdown should move from ~19 s to
  roughly 1 s. The 0.1 s steps before 17 s would jump over it. Use a small step from the start, e.g.
  `maxEventDt` 0.002 until ~3 s, then 0.01, and put the fine VTK output over that window. Breakdown then
  comes with much less cooling and precipitate behind it; what matters is the growth phase after it.
- **For 1b,** the crack band will be roughly twice as wide, while `damageDependenceConstant` = 8 and area 2000 were
  sized for a ~1 mm band. If 1b slows the crack, a follow-up can rescale them (c ≈ 7.3, area ≈ 1000).
  Expect a later, higher breakdown (size effect; an earlier l = 0.5 mm Biot run broke down at
  about 20.2 MPa and later) and a wider crack band. Keep the dt switch before breakdown and extend maxTime
  if needed.

### Stage 2: which part of the AS effect is mechanical and which is clogging

This needs a small code change. In `EigenstrainReactiveSolidUpdates::smallStrainUpdateChemoMechanicsFixedStress`,
the precipitated volume increment Δv is subtracted from each of the three normal strains (`computeTotalStress`),
so the volumetric eigenstrain is 3Δv. Add an input, e.g. `reactionEigenstrainScale` (default 1, keeping current
behaviour), that multiplies `anelasticStrainIncrement`. Then:

| test | setting | question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2a | scale 0 (no crystallisation stress) | Does the breakdown delay in pair 2 disappear? If so it's mechanical, not clogging. |
| 2b | scale 1/3 (volumetric strain = Δv) | How big is the effect with the volume-consistent strain? |

Run on the AS deck only; the water control has no precipitate and is unaffected.

### Stage 3: keep the crack contained

This depends on what the 75 mm edge stands for. **Still to be decided by Frank.**

- **A cut-off of a larger rock mass:** use a larger domain (e.g. 300 mm; needs a new mesh), or as a stopgap
  remove the `outerPressure` face condition so the edge is no-flow. A no-flow edge has its own artefact: a
  crack that reaches it can't extend, so pressure rises in both runs. The water control subtracts it, but
  results after that point are artificial.
- **The free surface of a lab sample:** keep p = 0; the screenout then has to form before breakthrough, so
  rely on Stage 1.

### Stage 4: push toward a tip screenout (after Stages 1–3 give a clean window)

| test | change | question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4a | reactive area 5000–10000 in `_caseC.xml` | Precipitation is kinetics-limited, so θ should grow faster. Is there a threshold where borehole pressure turns upward? |
| 4b | cooling front that moves with the fluid (4D table from `make_cooling_field.py`) | Today the prescribed cold zone keeps the clog at 6–20 mm while the tip is 50–70 mm out, a near-wellbore choke. Can the clog follow the tip? |
| 4c | the dehydration water sink (planned for this branch) | The other route to tip-localised supersaturation. |

### Later: check that a positive result is robust

- A mesh strip refined to h ≈ l/2 along the y-axis (the crack path is known by symmetry), or an l sensitivity study.
- Crack porosity from the crack opening instead of damage alone.

## Open questions

1. What does the 75 mm outer edge represent? This decides Stage 3.
2. The reaction eigenstrain: is 3Δv intended? And should precipitate filling open pore space stress the
   skeleton at all before the pore is full (crystallisation pressure)? This effect currently produces the
   only clear AS signal, the breakdown delay.
3. Fluid compressibility 1e-8 was presumably chosen for robustness. If 1a runs cleanly at 4.5e-10, adopt
   that value from then on.
